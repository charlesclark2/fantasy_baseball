# 4. Exact Live-Product Claims That Must Stay Consistent

These are factual contracts.

## 4.1 Models-Agree Indicator

If the product displays a "models agree" indicator:

> It means two independent Credence models produced close estimates.

It is computed **without using the betting market**.

It does **not** mean:

- high betting confidence,
- strong market edge,
- best bet,
- high-conviction wager.

## 4.2 Market Consensus

The market comparison is:

> A plain, unweighted, vig-removed average across sportsbooks that price both sides of the game.

Rules:

- No sportsbook receives greater weight.
- No sharp/soft weighting.
- Individual books may differ from the displayed consensus.
- FAQ language must match this implementation exactly.

## 4.3 Featured MLB Read

The featured MLB item is:

> A transparent example of where the model and market disagree.

It is not:

- the best bet,
- the day's biggest edge,
- a guaranteed recommendation,
- the highest expected-value wager.

## 4.4 MLB Track Record

Credence publishes a public MLB track record.

The correct message is:

> We grade the model against the market publicly, including losses, and the historical record has not demonstrated a durable advantage over the closing market.

Do not remove or soften that admission.

# 6. Section 1 — Why Credence Exists

## Heading

**Why Credence exists**

## Copy

Sports analytics is full of precise-looking numbers.

A player is projected for 17.4 fantasy points.

A team has a 61% chance to win.

A market supposedly has a 4% edge.

The number is rarely the whole answer.

How uncertain is it? What assumptions produced it? How often has the model been right before? Does the apparent difference survive proper testing? What should you actually do differently because of it?

Credence Sports exists to make those questions part of the product rather than something hidden behind the model.

> **We are not trying to eliminate uncertainty from sports. We are trying to measure it well enough to make better decisions inside it.**

# 8. Section 3 — What We Believe

## 01 — Show the uncertainty.

Every estimate should carry a range, distribution, or other uncertainty measure when the model supports one.

A wide interval is information, not a flaw. It tells the user how much confidence the evidence warrants.

## 02 — Grade the whole record.

We publish wins and losses rather than only the observations that went well.

Our MLB track record is public, and it states plainly that the model has not demonstrated a durable advantage over the closing market.

That is not something to hide.

It is part of the evidence.

## 03 — Show the work.

A projection is more useful when the user can understand what moved it.

Where possible, expose:

- Inputs.
- Model drivers.
- Market comparison.
- Uncertainty.
- Evaluation history.

## 04 — Retire what fails.

Models and hypotheses should clear evaluation gates defined before the answer is known.

If an idea does not survive proper testing, close it rather than changing the test until it passes.

## 05 — Separate disagreement from edge.

A model disagreeing with a market or consensus does not prove the model is correct.

Credence distinguishes between:

```text
model-market disagreement
evidence of signal
actionable edge
```

and does not treat those as interchangeable.

# 11. Section 5 — How We Judge Ourselves

## Heading

**The model does not get to grade its own homework.**

## Copy

Every serious model change is tested against explicit benchmarks and held-out data.

New features have to beat simpler alternatives. More complex models have to justify their complexity. Calibration matters. So does whether a result survives reasonable validation.

When a result is underpowered, we call it underpowered.

When a signal disappears under proper testing, we say so.

When the market remains better, we say that too.

> **Our goal is not to make every research question produce a winning model. It is to make the answer trustworthy.**

# 18. FAQ — Required Answers

## 18.1 What does Credence Sports actually do?

Recommended:

> Credence Sports builds fantasy decision tools and betting intelligence.
>
> For fantasy football, we provide draft rankings, player projections, projection ranges, and league-specific personalization.
>
> For MLB betting intelligence, we publish model probabilities, compare them with the betting market, show the uncertainty around those estimates, and maintain a public track record.
>
> Credence is an analytics and decision-support platform. It does not place bets on your behalf.

---

## 18.2 Why are Credence's projected points lower than other sites'?

**New required fantasy FAQ.**

Recommended:

> Credence's season projections are availability-weighted expected points.
>
> That means the projection includes the possibility that a player misses games. Many fantasy sites publish a number closer to "what this player would score if he plays the full season," which will naturally be higher.
>
> Credence is answering a different question:
>
> **How many points should we expect this player to produce across the season after accounting for both performance and the chance he is unavailable?**
>
> That is why some Credence projections may look lower even when the underlying per-game expectation is similar.
>
> If the product currently displays an "if healthy" or equivalent full-season figure, link or point to it here.
>
> Do not claim that this approach is more accurate than competitors unless that comparison has been directly validated.

---

## 18.3 What market are the MLB probabilities compared against?

Recommended exact description:

> Credence compares its model probability with a market-consensus probability calculated from sportsbooks that are currently pricing both sides of the game.
>
> For each eligible sportsbook, we remove the sportsbook's vig and then take a plain, unweighted average of the resulting probabilities.
>
> No sportsbook is weighted more heavily than another.
>
> Individual books can differ from the displayed consensus, so the price available to you may not match the consensus number exactly.

---

## 18.4 What does "models agree" mean?

Recommended:

> "Models agree" means two independent Credence models produced similar probability estimates.
>
> The indicator is calculated without using the betting market.
>
> It is a measure of agreement between the models — not a statement that a wager is high confidence, not proof of an edge, and not a guarantee that the predicted outcome will occur.

---

## 18.5 Does model-market disagreement mean there is an edge?

Recommended:

> No.
>
> A model-market disagreement tells you that Credence's estimate differs from the market-consensus estimate.
>
> That difference is useful information, but it does not prove that Credence is right or that a profitable betting edge exists.
>
> Our public MLB track record currently has not demonstrated a durable advantage over the closing market, and we publish that result openly.

---

## 18.6 What is the featured MLB read?

Recommended:

> The featured MLB read highlights a game where the model and the market disagree in a way that is useful to inspect.
>
> It is presented for transparency and explanation.
>
> It is not labeled as the day's "best bet," "biggest edge," or guaranteed recommendation.

---

## 18.7 Model % vs Market %

Recommended:

> Model % is the probability Credence assigns to an outcome.
>
> Market % is the vig-removed consensus probability derived from sportsbooks pricing both sides of the game.
>
> The gap shows where the two estimates disagree.
>
> It should be interpreted as disagreement, not automatic evidence of value or edge.

## 18.10 What does "Preliminary" mean?

Recommended:

> Preliminary MLB projections are generated before confirmed lineups are available and therefore rely on projected players and available roster information.
>
> Once official lineups are posted, the model is rerun with confirmed players and the estimate may change.
>
> "Preliminary" describes the information state of the projection. It is not a confidence rating.
